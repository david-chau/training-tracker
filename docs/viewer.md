# Viewer guide

Someone sent you a view-only link — your trainer, or whoever keeps the log.
It shows the training history. There is nothing to install and no account to
make.

---

## Opening it

Tap the link. You will see the current session for whichever day you pick —
Push, Pull or Legs — with every set, the weight, the reps and the RPE that
were recorded.

A grey badge at the top says **Read only — you can look, not change**. That is
normal. This link is for looking things up; the numbers are entered through
the admin link, which someone else holds.

{: .tip }
To keep it handy, open the link and use *Share → Add to Home Screen* on
iPhone or iPad, or the browser menu → *Add to Home screen* on Android. It
then opens like an app.

---

## Finding an old session

1. Tap the day type along the top — **Push**, **Pull**, **Legs**, or whatever
   names your log uses.
2. Use the **‹** arrow next to the date to step back through previous
   sessions of that type. **›** goes forward again.

Only day types that have sessions appear, so every button leads somewhere. If
you see *"Nothing has been logged yet"*, nothing has been recorded in this log
at all so far.

If a date has nothing logged you will see *No Push session logged on…* — that
just means you did not train that day.

## Reading a card

```
Barbell Bench Press                       4 sets
Last time: 10 x 20 @8 · 8 x 30 @9

     Reps    Weight (LB)    RPE
 1     12         25          8
 2     10         30          9
```

- **Reps** — how many you did.
- **Weight (LB)** — in pounds.
- **RPE** — how hard the set felt, out of 10. A dash means it was not
  recorded.
- **Last time** — the same sets from your previous session of that day, so you
  can see the progression.

Any note left against an exercise appears underneath it.

---

## What RPE means

RPE is how hard a set felt, on a scale of 1 to 10:

| RPE | You could have done… |
|---|---|
| 10 | nothing more |
| 9 | one more rep |
| 8 | two more reps |
| 7 | three more reps |
| 6 or less | plenty more |

It matters because next week's weights are worked out from it. A set that felt
like a 6 gets more weight next time; a set at 10 gets backed off. Being honest
about it is the whole mechanism.

---

## A few practical things

- **Your link is personal.** It shows your training history to anyone who
  opens it, so treat it like any other private link and do not post it
  publicly.
- **It needs a connection.** Gym wifi permitting, the page loads live from
  the spreadsheet each time. There is no offline copy.
- **You cannot break anything.** The link genuinely cannot save changes, even
  if you try.
- **Something looks wrong?** Tell whoever holds the admin link — they can fix
  it in a couple of taps.
